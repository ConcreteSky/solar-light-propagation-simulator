# Radiation and charged-particle environment

The `particleEnvironment` records describe solar-wind exposure, trapped-particle context, local interaction type, and qualitative surface exposure. The labels `low`, `moderate`, `high`, `extreme`, and `unknown` compare broad environments; they are not absorbed dose, biological risk, electronics lifetime, or an operational forecast.

Photons and charged particles remain separate throughout the interface. Visible light uses a solid yellow beam and the original transmitted/scattered/absorbed energy partition. Charged particles use a dotted cyan stream and magnetic interaction schematic. A magnetic field can redirect or trap charged particles, while an atmosphere can add shielding; neither is represented as changing the visible-light fractions unless the existing atmosphere model does so.

Jupiter's environment is classified as extreme and its inner moons inherit parent-magnetosphere exposure. Titan is variable because it can move through different regions of Saturn's interaction environment. Unknown small-body environments remain unknown.
